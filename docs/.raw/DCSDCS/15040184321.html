Introduction

Accessibility means making sure that everyone can perceive, understand, navigate, contribute and interact with a Solution.

This means making sure the Solution can be used by people with a wide range of different abilities, disabilities or impairments. Accessibility is both a clinical and legal risk, which extends to Suppliers.

It's a legal requirement for the Authority as a public sector body to ensure that it’s Solutions are accessible. Non-public sector bodies are also obliged to make their websites accessible under the Equality Act 2010.

ACS001

Web Content Accessibility Guidelines

Conformance with legislation The Public Sector Bodies Accessibility Regulations 2018 demonstrated by:

The Solution to meet Conformance Level AA of the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG 2.2) or higher, on the following services:

  • websites and all their content

  • any software which uses the web browser as the interface

  • extranets

  • applications for mobile devices, such as phones or tablets

ACS002

Web Content Accessibility Testing

Conduct a Web Content Accessibility Guidelines audit every 12 months for the Solution.

The audit has to be carried out by a 3rd party G-Cloud approved auditor and assessed to WCAG 2.2. Audit to include outcomes of testing with the assistive technologies outlined on GOV.UK guidance on testing with assistive technologies.

Audit will include the most recent features created in the Solution since the previous audit

For further information on arranging an accessibility audit please see Getting an accessibility audit on Gov.uk Service manual.

In the event that the Supplier is also a G-Cloud certified auditor, the Supplier must seek a different auditor.

ACS004

Web Content Accessibility User Research

People with access needs, including disabled people, to be involved in every round of user research to assess if the Solution is accessible in the context of use as well as technically accessible.

For more information see Accessibility guidance for User research in the NHS Digital Service Manual.

ACS005

Accessible non-web Based Material

The Supplier to provide on demand to users of the Solution all non-web based material in multiple accessible formats e.g. physical manuals, Word documents and PDFs are made available in accessible formats.

The Supplier to provide multiple accessible methods of requesting non-web based materials in accessible formats.

Clear contact information of an email address and phone number as a minimum to be provided by Suppliers to enable Users to submit requests

  • Documentation of contact information for Users to submit requests

ASC006

Declaration of Disproportionate Burden

The Supplier to declare up front any considerations identified as “Disproportionate Burden” and reaffirm declaration where there is no change every 12 months.

The Supplier to declare and contact the Authority if and when any and all changes to an existing Declaration of Disproportionate Burden take place within an accessibility statement.

For more information see Understanding accessibility requirements for public sector bodies - GOV.UK

MUST

Self-certification with Supporting Evidence

Supporting evidence must include:

  • A clearly documented declaration and justification for considerations identified as Disproportionate Burden

Supporting evidence may include:

  • A thorough and transparent report including financial figures and the impact on the users.

  • A report signed off by a legal professional proving the report is lawful and aligns with the legislation.

  • Annually provide legal evidence reaffirming the declaration and will be open to freedom of information requests.
